# PRD Quality Review — Contax

## Overall verdict
This is a strong, unusually disciplined chain-top PRD: it carries a real thesis (invert the burden of proof; wedge = weekly report, moat = anti-fraud depth + data), names its trade-offs instead of smoothing them (CV kept against research advice, Source B kept despite friction, a deliberately large MVP), and — rare — preserves reviewer dissent rather than laundering it into consensus (§15). Most FRs carry genuinely testable "Consequences," which puts done-ness above the typical bar. What's at risk is downstream fidelity, not strategy: three broken FR cross-references will actively mislead story extraction, FR-17's status contradicts its own re-file in §7.1/§15, the role→permission/authority matrix that story creation needs most is never tabulated, and several anomaly-rule thresholds are left as bare variables. None are strategic; all are fixable. The document is pilot-recruitment-ready and close to build-ready once the mechanical drift and the permission matrix are closed.

## Decision-readiness — strong
The value hierarchy is stated as a decision, not a consideration: §0 — "when the two conflict, the company wins." Trade-offs are named with what was given up: the CV progress-% call is flagged against advice (FR-17 `[NOTE FOR PM]`, R-3), Source B is kept "despite collection friction" (§4.5, R-7), and the "deliberately large" MVP is owned as an examined call (§7.1) *and* dissented (D-1, RR-4). The 15 Open Questions are real tickets with owners, not rhetorical. `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts land on actual tensions (CV reliability, spot-check prompts). A decision-maker pushing back on scope or CV would find their objection already registered in §15, not dodged.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-17 carries two contradictory statuses (§4.3 vs §7.1/§15) — FR-17's body/NOTE still says CV is "retained as a v1 MUST by owner's call," while §7.1 and RR-2 re-file it to a "Shadow-Mode research spike... **not** a build-blocking MUST." A reader must reconcile which state governs. *Fix:* update FR-17's prose to the re-filed status (shadow spike, not MUST) and point to RR-2/Open Q15, so the FR and the register agree.

## Substance over theater — strong
Little furniture here. The Vision (§1) is specific to Pakistani construction — suspicion as the client's defining experience, the site engineer as both reassurer and cheat — and could not swap into another PRD. NFRs carry product-specific bounds, not boilerplate: field-level redaction (NFR-5), self-hosted-only fraud math (NFR-4/NFR-12), templated numeric guardrails (NFR-6). Counter-metrics (SM-C1..C3) are genuinely adversarial — SM-C1 names "capture rate up, credibility down" as a fraud-laundering signal, which is the opposite of vanity metrics. Differentiation is honest rather than inflated: §12 concedes "the mechanics are table-stakes; the differentiation is positioning and, later, data," and §M/§R-8 explicitly flags two premise claims (material theft, overseas PKR volume) as unverified and instructs the reader not to overstate them.

### Findings
- **low** Six UJ personas sit at the rubric's persona-count ceiling — each maps to a distinct real stakeholder (Owner, Client, and four operator roles) and drives specific FRs, so they're earned, not theater; noted only as a threshold to defend against future additions.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The PRD has a clear arc and the features serve it: the three-capture spine (§4.2) is the root, everything else is a computed projection, and the invariants (§9) restate the thesis as engineering constraints ("adversarial toward its own operators by construction"). Prioritization follows the thesis, not ease — SM-1 tests the single load-bearing assumption (the mandate carries honest capture) and everything else is declared "moot if this fails." Counter-metrics exist for each primary/secondary metric. The MVP scope kind (problem-solving + trust-experience) is coherent.

### Findings
- **low** Thesis-vs-scope tension: the thesis prizes a *minimal* closed capture spine, yet the MVP is *maximal* (57 FRs, zero signed pilots). The PRD self-acknowledges this (D-1, RR-4 registered dissent, post-pilot stack audit as a named gate), which is why coherence holds — the bigness is examined, not accidental. Noted, not a defect.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
The FR-level "Consequences (testable)" pattern is a real strength — most FRs give a verifiable condition (FR-6 "quantity/rate fields populated by OCR... not required to type," FR-19 "no AP entry commits without an Accountant sign-off," FR-45 "a gap in the sequence is visible"). Being unforgiving, though, the artifacts story creation leans on hardest are the under-specified ones: there is no consolidated role→permission grid, and several anomaly rules are stated with undefined parameters. Deferred thresholds that *are* flagged to Open Questions (cash-heavy %, dispute window, retention horizon, CV exit criteria) are handled honestly and don't count against done-ness; the ones below are gaps that aren't fully closed.

### Findings
- **medium** No role→permission/authority matrix. FR-2 asserts each Operator gets "exactly one role... that governs what they may capture, view, and approve," but the actual role×action grid exists only as scattered examples (Storekeeper can't sign AP; raiser can't ratify). Story creation and access-control implementation (NFR-9, NFR-18) need the full grid. *Fix:* add a permission/authority-tier table enumerating capture/view/approve/ratify/bill rights per role.
- **medium** Anomaly-rule thresholds left as bare variables. FR-36 ships rules "payment with no Challan/photo within **N days**" and "purchase rate exceeds Rate Book by **> X%**," and FR-34 uses "a configurable margin" — but N, X, and the CO margin have no defaults and aren't all in Open Questions (only cash-heavy % is, Q3). An engineer cannot write a test for "flag when rate exceeds prior by X%" without X. *Fix:* give recommended defaults (per the §9 north-star) or file each as an Open Question.
- **low** FR-42 "self-explanatory to a stranger (a forward recipient with no context can understand it)" is not testable as written. *Fix:* restate as a checkable property (e.g. report contains project name, company brand, week number, and a plain-language summary block) or move it to a UX acceptance note.

## Scope honesty — strong
Omissions are explicit and do real work. §5 lists nine substantive Non-Goals with rationale (no supplier app, no in-app chat, no SMS/IVR capture, no client-family data entry, no segment fork), and §7.2 separates deferred-to-v2 cleanly. De-scoping is done in the open, not silently: the CV re-file is labeled "**re-filed, not deleted**" and gated on Owner sign-off (RR-2, Open Q15). Inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags round-trip to the §13.1 index. The one caveat is volume, not concealment.

### Findings
- **low** High open-items density for a document framed as feeding build: 15 Open Questions + 12 indexed assumptions + 8 risks + 4 register items. Every item is surfaced (the opposite of a hidden gap), but the aggregate means the PRD is genuinely *pilot-recruitment-ready*, not green-light-to-build — consistent with `status: ready-for-review` and R-2 (no signed pilot). *Fix:* none needed; just don't let a downstream reader treat it as build-final while Q1/Q6/Q14 are open.

## Downstream usability — adequate
This is a chain-top PRD (feeds UX → architecture → epics), so source-extractability matters most, and the foundations are good: the Glossary is disciplined and instructs verbatim reuse; FR (1..57), NFR (1..20), UJ (1..6), and SM IDs are contiguous and unique; every UJ has a named protagonist carrying context inline; each feature section reads standalone. What drags it from strong is that three cross-references resolve to the wrong FR — the single worst failure mode for a source-extracting workflow, because a wrong pointer silently produces a wrong story — plus one genuine semantic contradiction about who holds disclosure authority.

### Findings
- **medium** Three broken FR cross-references. (1) FR-8 "corrections create a new versioned entry... (see FR-24)" — FR-24 is Source B capture; the append-only-ledger target is **FR-23**. (2) FR-15 "Consumption exceeding BOQ-remaining raises an Anomaly Flag (see FR-42)" — FR-42 is the weekly PDF; the BOQ-overconsumption rule is **FR-36**. (3) FR-40 "disclosure requires a Management action (see FR-52)" — FR-52 is voice-note ASR; disclosure control is **FR-53**. *Fix:* repoint all three; they read like references written before renumbering.
- **medium** Disclosure-authority inconsistency. The Glossary (Owner "holds... disclosure discretion"), §9, and UJ-5 (Rana the Owner "decides who sees it") vest anomaly-disclosure discretion in the Owner/Management, but FR-53 says "**A Manager** can decide, per Anomaly Flag, whether it is disclosed." Manager and Owner are distinct Glossary roles; access-control implementation needs one answer. *Fix:* state explicitly which role(s) hold disclosure rights (and fold into the permission matrix above).

## Shape fit — strong
The PRD is a multi-stakeholder B2B product with meaningful UX on both sides (low-literacy operator capture; overseas-client trust surface), so UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing — and it has exactly six, each earning its place, neither over- nor under-formalized. It deliberately straddles two shapes — a client-facing trust *experience* and an internal anti-fraud *capability spec* — and §1 frames that duality head-on rather than being forced into one. Chain-top status is matched by real traceability discipline (Glossary-anchored, ID-numbered, UJ/FR/SM cross-links). RR-4's "infrastructure is heavy" dissent is a scope-weight concern, not a shape mismatch.
